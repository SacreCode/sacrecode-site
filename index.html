Fig. 01The problem

Everything is starting to look like _everything _else.

AI can now produce a million passable images before breakfast. Styles get cloned overnight. Trends eat themselves in a week. The result is symbolic saturation: a world full of pictures and empty of surprise.

In that world, the question is no longer “is this beautiful?” or even “is this real?” — it is: could this have come from anyone, or only from you?

Fig. 02The idea, in plain words

Every work carries a _symbolic _DNA.

  • 01

    It has layers.

    The references, memories, beliefs and choices inside it — everything it quietly says beyond what it shows.

  • 02

    Most of them are common.

    Shared tropes, familiar styles, borrowed moods. A machine trained on the whole internet reproduces them effortlessly.

  • 03

    Some are rare. We measure that.

    Lived experience, private mythology, one person’s angle on the world. Our measure of it is called Δsim.

Fig. 04How it works

Three steps. _No _jargon.

01 · Decode

Read the layers.

We read the work’s symbolic structure — its motifs, references and emotional registers — the way a geologist reads layers of rock.

02 · Compare

Measure the distance.

How close could the nearest machine-made neighbour get? Not “did AI make this?” — but “could it?” That distance is Δsim.

03 · Map

Get the map.

A plain-language report: where your work is rare, where it is derivative, and what carries its resonance over time.

Fig. 06 · Why we do this

Human authenticity is not nostalgia. It is _infrastructure.

We are advocates for preserving the human DNA of culture in a time of AI saturation. Not against the machines — alongside them, with a clear line drawn: this part is common, and this part is a person.
